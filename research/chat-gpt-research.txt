If I were starting from zero and wanted to build my own AI-assisted trading system for Indian markets, I would not begin with an AI model that predicts BUY/SELL.

I would build a reliable trading platform first, prove that simple strategies work after realistic costs, then add AI where it has a measurable advantage.

That order matters.

As of November 2026, retail API algo trading in India now sits under SEBI’s newer retail algo framework. SEBI’s framework and exchange implementation standards require broker-mediated API access, and NSE’s standards include static-IP mapping for trading API access. Securities and Exchange Board of India

Stage 9 — Prevent backtesting fraud against yourself
These errors are deadly.
Look-ahead bias
Using information that wasn't available yet.

Example:
today_close


to make a decision supposedly executed before today's close.

Wrong.
Survivorship bias
Backtesting today's NIFTY constituents on old history.

You accidentally remove companies that failed or left the index.
Overfitting
Trying thousands of combinations until one looks amazing.

Example:
EMA 18
EMA 47
RSI 57
ATR 1.83
volume 1.31x

producing unbelievable historical returns.

Very likely curve fitting.
Data leakage
Future information enters your ML training data.

ML can produce spectacular fake performance because of this.

Stage 12 — Build the risk engine BEFORE live trading
I would not let strategies directly call the broker.

Architecture:
Strategy
   ↓
Signal
   ↓
RISK ENGINE
   ↓

   ↓
Broker

Risk engine decides whether the trade is allowed.

Example:
if daily_loss > MAX_DAILY_LOSS:    reject_trade()if open_positions >= MAX_POSITIONS:    reject_trade()if risk_per_trade > capital * 0.005:    reject_trade()if system_drawdown > MAX_DRAWDOWN:    disable_strategy()


Possible initial rules:
Risk/trade          0.25–0.50%
Max daily loss      1%
Max portfolio risk  3%
Max strategy DD     8–12%
Max positions       5

Those are engineering examples, not personalized financial recommendations.
Stage 13 — Position sizing
Suppose:
Capital = ₹5,00,000

Risk/trade = 0.5%

Risk = ₹2,500

Trade:
Entry = ₹1,000
Stop  = ₹975

Risk/share = ₹25

Then:
position size =
₹2,500 / ₹25

= 100 shares

This is far more disciplined than:
"I feel confident, buy ₹2 lakh."

One current regulatory point you should design around from day one: India's retail algo framework is now materially different from the old "just call the broker API from your laptop" model. SEBI's framework and NSE implementation standards put broker/exchange controls around retail algo access, and static-IP requirements now affect order-placement integrations. Securities and Exchange Board of India
